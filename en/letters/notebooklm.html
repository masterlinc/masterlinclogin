NotebookLM: The Real Value Is Not Making Your PPT

Field note from management · 2026-08

I used to think AI output sounded fake because the model wasn't strong enough.

Then I realized: most of the time it's not the model. I gave it one throwaway instruction, yet expected it to understand my business like a colleague who'd worked with me for three years.

I wrote a comment on a NotebookLM post: don't chase longer prompts first — put the context of one type of task together first. That comment got 207 likes and 7 replies. Some asked how, some asked whether it costs money.

I now use only four steps:

  1. Handle one task type at a time — a weekly report, meeting minutes, or one SOP;
  2. Have AI separate "facts confirmed in the material" from "gaps with no basis";
  3. Draft by "input — judgment — output — needs human confirmation";
  4. Save what I changed and the final decision as context for the next round.

The real value is not more material. It's AI being able to see why I edit, and where it cannot decide for me.

When material is sensitive, I don't upload everything. I remove names, amounts and client identifiers first, and run one week on just 10–20 desensitized samples of the same type.

AI sounds fake because it doesn't know your work.

Last time AI said "everything is right but useless" — was it in a weekly report, a meeting, or a proposal?
